Gameplay

Every Turn Is a Small Puzzle

How It Plays

Cards, Dice and a Map That Fights Back

Draft Your Squad

Every pilot is human plus two animal bloodlines. Build yours from eleven bloodlines and 99 trait cards, or start with a ready-made hero, and grow each kit from six cards to nine as the expedition goes on.

Two Faces on Every Card

A card’s Physical face is cheap and hits harder as you train it. Its Ethereal face spends an etherion of Mass, Charge, Space or Time to do what a body can’t. Either way, the card cools down on a six-column track before you can play it again.

The Enemy Shows Its Hand

Before you act, every enemy declares what it is about to do. Each turn you roll four dice from a pool of fifteen, one of them an element die you pick, and you choose who to shield, who to strike and where to stand. Nothing interrupts your plan, and attacks never roll to hit.

Not Every Problem Is a Fight

Channel a valve before the flood, break the seals on a ledge, hold a beacon through the night. Every board is generated fresh, with its own gravity, wind, fog and time of day.

A Route Across Tumult

Cross a branching map of missions, havens and events by land, sea and air. Train between stops, pick up gear, and pick your difficulty: Steady wind, or Gale when you want it harder.

Alone or Around the Table

Fly the whole squad yourself, or bring up to five players: pass one screen around, or host a table online and share its code. Take turns, or all play the round at once.

The World

Gravity Is a Geography

Tumult is a built world about ten times the surface of Earth. Near its poles gravity is crushing; near the equator it is a tenth of Earth’s. People say uphill for toward the heavy poles and downhill for toward the light equator, whatever the ground is doing.

A day lasts about an hour. The wind never stops, so windbreaks are infrastructure, and the ground itself collapses and is rebuilt. Every person is human plus two animal clades, and they get things done three ways: what their bodies can do, the etherions that power magic, and gear that runs on omnicrystals.

Tumult shares its pilots and its four elements with Cosmic Shore, Froglet’s party game for pilots.

  • Massbodies and force: push, pull, weight, anchoring
  • Chargeenergy and links: shock, fields, chains, sensing
  • Spaceposition and shape: reach, area, swapping, height
  • Timetempo: haste, delay, stasis, foresight, recovery
Quick Answers

Good to Know

Is Tumult free?

Yes. The whole game is free to play, with no purchases inside it.

Do I need to download or install anything?

No. Tumult plays in your web browser on a phone, tablet or computer. After your first visit it keeps its files, so it starts quickly next time.

Do I need an account?

No. Anyone can play right away, alone or passing one screen around, without signing in. Sign in later if you want to keep your squad and your saves on any device.

Where is my progress saved?

In your browser, on your device. Clearing your browser’s site data for Tumult also clears your expedition, unless you have signed in to keep it.

How long is a game?

A mission lasts up to about ten rounds. A full expedition, from the draft to the end of the route, is a longer session that you can stop and resume at any point.

Who makes it?

Froglet Games, the studio behind Cosmic Shore. Tumult is open source: its code is on GitHub.
